Understanding Microwave Power Requirements

Microwaves are known for being power-hungry appliances. They typically have two key wattage ratings you need to consider:

  • Running Wattage: This is the power the microwave consumes while actively heating food. Most household microwaves range from 600 to 1500 watts.
  • Peak (Surge) Wattage: When a microwave first starts, it draws a brief burst of higher power, known as surge wattage. This can be 1.5 to 2 times its running wattage. Your power station must be able to handle this initial surge.

For example, an 800-watt microwave might have a peak wattage of 1200-1600 watts. Always check the label on your microwave or its manual for precise specifications.

Estimating Runtime: How Long Will It Last?

To calculate the approximate runtime, use this simple formula:

(Jackery Battery Capacity in Wh * 0.85) / Appliance Wattage in W = Estimated Hours

The 0.85 factor accounts for power conversion efficiency (typically around 85% for AC output).

Example: Running an 800W microwave with a Jackery Explorer 2000 Pro (2160Wh):
(2160 Wh * 0.85) / 800 W = 2.295 hours (approx. 2 hours and 18 minutes)
